Earthquake visualizer

An interactive web visualisation of significant earthquakes and tsunamis, in which a map, a timeline, a scatterplot and a detail panel filter each other.

Overview

The application lets people explore historical records of significant earthquakes and the tsunamis they caused. Four views are linked, so a selection in any of them filters the others:

  • World map. Earthquakes as points over tectonic plate boundaries, with size and colour mapped to attributes such as magnitude, depth, intensity, deaths or damage, and a heatmap mode.
  • Timeline. When earthquakes happened, for choosing a period.
  • Scatterplot. Any two attributes against each other, magnitude against focal depth by default.
  • Detail view. The full record of one earthquake and any tsunamis it caused.

Preset points of interest lead into notable patterns, such as low-magnitude earthquakes that caused a lot of damage, and a filter restricts every view to earthquakes that caused a tsunami.

My part: the timeline

The records span several thousand years and are very unevenly distributed, so a simple histogram over time does not work. I built the timeline as a grid: rows are ranges of years, columns are months, and each cell is coloured by the number of earthquakes.

  • The year ranges adapt to the data, and selecting a range zooms into it with finer ranges; a back button returns to the previous level, including for periods with sparse records.
  • Dragging a box over cells selects a timeframe, highlights the cells and filters the map, scatterplot and detail view to the selected earthquakes.
  • Many historical records have no month, so they get their own column instead of being dropped.

Data

The earthquake and tsunami records were exported as tab-separated files and converted to GeoJSON with a small Python script that keeps every row with valid coordinates as a point feature.
