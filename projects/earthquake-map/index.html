Data Visualisation · TU Delft

Earthquake visualizer

An interactive web visualisation of significant earthquakes and tsunamis, in which a map, a timeline, a scatterplot and a detail panel filter each other.

Try it live. Open the earthquake visualizer ↗

Overview

The application lets people explore historical records of 6,393 significant earthquakes and 2,512 tsunamis, from ancient records to 2025. It has four linked views, so a selection in any of them filters the others. The sections below walk through each view.

View 1 · my part

Timeline

The records span several thousand years and are very unevenly spread, so a simple histogram over time does not work. I built the timeline as a grid: rows are ranges of years, columns are months, and each cell is coloured by the number of earthquakes.

  • Dragging a box over cells selects a timeframe and filters the map, scatterplot and detail view to those earthquakes; the selection is shown in orange.
  • Clicking a year range zooms into it with finer ranges, and a back button returns to the previous level.
  • 402 records, mostly historical, have no month. They get their own Unknown column, set slightly apart, which can be selected like any month.
  • Colours follow a log scale, so year ranges with only a few records stay visible next to the busiest ones.

View 2

World map

Earthquakes are drawn as points over tectonic plate boundaries. Dot size and colour can be mapped to magnitude, focal depth, intensity, deaths, injuries, damage or houses destroyed, and colour can also show the country or whether the earthquake caused a tsunami.

  • A switch turns the points into a heatmap, and another shows only earthquakes that caused a tsunami.
  • Clicking or Ctrl-dragging a box on the map selects earthquakes there.
  • Here the map shows only the earthquakes from the timeline selection above.

View 3

Scatterplot

Any two attributes can be plotted against each other; by default it shows magnitude against focal depth, where most earthquakes cluster at shallow depths and a separate group of deep-focus earthquakes stands out.

  • Both axes can be switched to impact measures such as deaths or damage, on a log scale where needed.
  • Clicking or Ctrl-dragging selects earthquakes, which are then highlighted on the map and timeline.

Points of interest and details

Three preset points of interest lead into notable patterns: earthquakes with many people missing, earthquakes with many houses destroyed, and low-magnitude earthquakes that caused a lot of damage. Each preset comes with a short explanation and a link to official information about the event. Selecting a single earthquake shows its full record in the detail panel, including its location, magnitude, depth, impact figures and any tsunamis it caused.

Data

The earthquake and tsunami records were exported as tab-separated files and converted to GeoJSON with a small Python script that keeps every row with valid coordinates as a point feature. The application is built with D3 for the timeline, scatterplot and detail view, OpenLayers for the map and heatmap, and Vite for builds.
