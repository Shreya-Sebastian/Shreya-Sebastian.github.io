Data Visualisation · TU Delft

Earthquake visualizer

An interactive web visualisation of significant earthquakes and tsunamis, in which a map, a timeline, a scatterplot and a detail panel filter each other.

Try it live. Open the earthquake visualizer ↗

Overview

An interactive visualisation of 6,393 significant earthquakes and 2,512 tsunamis, from ancient records to 2025, built with D3 for the timeline, scatterplot and detail view, OpenLayers for the map and heatmap, and Vite for builds. Four coordinated views use linked brushing: a selection in any view filters the other three.

Coordinated views

Each view exposes a draw and an update operation; a selection in one view passes the selected records to the update of the others, and each applies them in its own way:

Receiving viewWhat it does with a selection
World mapShows only the selected earthquakes, in both the point and the heatmap layer.
ScatterplotKeeps every earthquake as context and draws the selection on top.
TimelineRebuilds its grid from the selection, adapting its year ranges to the selection's span.
Detail viewShows the full record of the first selected earthquake.

View 1 · my part

Timeline

The records span several thousand years and are very unevenly distributed, so a histogram over time does not work. I built the timeline as a heatmap grid of year ranges against months, with adaptive binning: range width follows the span of the data shown, from 500 years down to centuries, decades and single years.

  • Brushing a block of cells selects a timeframe and filters the other views, with a status line reporting the selection size.
  • Clicking a year range drills down into finer ranges, with history-based back navigation.
  • Records without a month (402, mostly historical) get their own selectable Unknown column.
  • Cell colour uses a log-scaled sequential encoding, so sparse ranges stay visible next to dense ones, with a contrasting palette for selections.

After the course I extended the timeline in my own fork, adding the log-scaled colours, selection feedback and the Unknown column.

View 2

World map

An OpenLayers map stacks an OpenStreetMap base layer, tectonic plate boundaries, a heatmap layer and the earthquake points. Dot size and colour can encode magnitude, focal depth, intensity or impact measures, with linear scales for magnitude and intensity and logarithmic scales for depth and impact.

  • A switch replaces the points with a magnitude-weighted heatmap; another filters to tsunami-causing earthquakes.
  • Click and box selection, including boxes across the 180th meridian, feed the linked views.
  • Here the map shows only the earthquakes from the timeline selection above.

View 3

Scatterplot

Any two attributes can be plotted against each other. The default, magnitude against focal depth, shows a dense cluster at shallow depths and a separate group of deep-focus earthquakes.

  • Axes for skewed impact measures such as deaths or damage switch to a D3 symmetric log scale with ticks at powers of ten.
  • Click and box selection highlight earthquakes on the map and timeline, and the selection persists across axis changes.

Points of interest and details

Three preset points of interest guide the user to notable patterns: earthquakes with many people missing, with many houses destroyed, and low-magnitude earthquakes with high damage. Each selects one event across all four views, sets matching scatterplot axes and links to official information. The detail panel shows the full record of a selected earthquake and joins it to its related tsunami records.

Data and deployment

The earthquake and tsunami records were exported as tab-separated files and converted to GeoJSON point features in Python. The site is built with Vite and deployed to GitHub Pages through continuous deployment.
