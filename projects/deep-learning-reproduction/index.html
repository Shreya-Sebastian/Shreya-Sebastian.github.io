Deep Learning · TU Delft

Deep learning paper reproduction

A reproduction of “Multi-view underwater image enhancement method via embedded fusion mechanism” for the CS4240 Deep Learning course.

Overview

Underwater photos lose colour and contrast because water absorbs and scatters light. The paper we reproduced enhances them with a network that fuses several versions of the same image. There was no public code, so we rebuilt the method from the paper's description and trained it on the UIEB benchmark.

Inputs

Each raw image is turned into three views: the original, a white-balanced version and a contrast-enhanced version (CLAHE on the lightness channel). Images are resized to 256 × 256, and the 890 image pairs are split 85/15 into training and test sets.

The model I built

  • Residual enhancement modules. Each view first passes through its own stack of residual blocks.
  • Multi-feature fusion. Fusion modules combine the views with 1 × 1, 3 × 3 and 5 × 5 convolutions and residual connections.
  • Multi-scale attention. Features are produced at three scales, and same-scale features are combined with a channel attention module that learns how much to weight each view.
  • Reconstruction. Further residual stages and upsampling produce the enhanced image.

Training used an L1 loss plus a perceptual loss on VGG-16 features, with Adam, for about 12 hours on a single GPU.

Where the paper was ambiguous

  • Channel counts between modules were not specified, so we added 1 × 1 convolutions to match them.
  • Parts of the architecture figure were open to interpretation; we implemented them as shared-kernel convolutions with downsampling.
  • The channel shuffle step was left out, as its backward pass was not available in PyTorch.

What replicated

The enhanced images come out visually close to the references. The quantitative scores did not line up with the paper's: PSNR and SSIM came out lower than reported, while PCQI, UCIQE and UIQM came out far higher. The write-up attributes the gap to possible differences in image size, training procedure or metric implementation. The write-up recommends a separate validation set for model selection and testing on a second dataset.
