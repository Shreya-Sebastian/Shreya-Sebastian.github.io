Deep Learning · TU Delft

Deep learning paper reproduction

A reproduction of “Multi-view underwater image enhancement method via embedded fusion mechanism” for the CS4240 Deep Learning course.

Overview

Underwater photos lose colour and contrast because water absorbs and scatters light. The paper we reproduced (Zhou et al., 2023) enhances them with MFEF, a multi-view fusion network. With no public code available, we reimplemented the method in PyTorch from the paper's description and trained it on the UIEB benchmark. This was a team of four for the TU Delft Deep Learning course; I built the model architecture, contributed to preprocessing and wrote the blog post.

Inputs

Each raw image is turned into three views: the original, a white-balanced version that corrects the colour shift caused by water, and a CLAHE version that equalises contrast on the lightness channel. Images are resized to 256 × 256, and the 890 image pairs are split 85/15 into training and test sets.

Architecture

original, white-balanced, CLAHE views
  → residual enhancement module per view
  → multi-feature fusion
  → second residual stage, added back onto each view
  → shared convolutions at three scales
  → pixel-weighted channel attention per scale
  → coarse-to-fine reconstruction → enhanced image
  • Residual enhancement. Each view passes through its own stack of residual blocks of 3 × 3 convolutions with skip connections.
  • Multi-feature fusion. Fusion modules combine the views with parallel 1 × 1, 3 × 3 and 5 × 5 convolution branches and residual connections. A second chain of residual modules follows, and each result is added pixel-wise to one of the input views.
  • Multi-scale channel attention. Shared-kernel convolutions with downsampling produce features at three scales. Same-scale maps from the three branches are concatenated and passed to a pixel-weighted channel attention module (PCAM), which combines per-pixel weighting with per-channel weighting from globally pooled features.
  • Reconstruction. Residual stages and upsampling rebuild the image from the coarsest scale up to full resolution.

Where the paper left details open, we made explicit design choices: 1 × 1 convolutions to match channel counts between modules, and concatenation of same-size outputs across branches for the unspecified dense connections.

Loss and training

Training used an L1 loss combined with a perceptual loss on the activations of an ImageNet-pretrained VGG-16, which captures texture, colour and structure that a pixel loss misses. The model was optimised with Adam for 200 epochs on a single GPU, and converged at epoch 89.

Results

The enhanced images come out visually close to the references. We evaluated with the paper's five metrics: PSNR and SSIM against the reference, PCQI for contrast, and the underwater-specific UCIQE and UIQM. SSIM was concentrated in a narrow range, indicating that structure is preserved consistently, while PSNR varied more between images. PSNR and SSIM were lower than reported in the paper, and the other three metrics did not line up with the paper's values.
