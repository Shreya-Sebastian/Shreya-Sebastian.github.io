RetroSTC-Net

Can an event that happens after learning decide whether a network keeps what it learned? Retroactive consolidation for neural networks, based on synaptic tagging and capture.

Background

The synaptic tagging and capture (STC) hypothesis (Frey and Morris, 1997) holds that learning leaves a short-lived change and a temporary tag, and the change becomes permanent only if plasticity-related products, which a separate salient event can trigger, arrive while the tag is present.

This project implements the mechanism in backprop-trained networks, tests for the behavioural-tagging time window and uses the consolidation record as an explanation of what the network retains. Continual learning methods such as Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) and Synaptic Intelligence (SI) fix a weight's importance at learning time, whereas importance often becomes clear only later, for instance when a fraud label arrives.

Building on related work on tag-and-capture and two-component weights (Leimer, Herzog and Senn, 2019; Li and van Rossum, 2020; Lehr, Luboeinski and Tetzlaff, 2022), this project adds:

  • retroactive consolidation in a backprop-trained network, decided by a salience event after learning;
  • a behavioural-tagging time-window test in network weights, including rescue of one task by another;
  • a consolidation log as an explanation of what was retained, validated with a faithfulness test.

Method

Each weight is split into a consolidated part L and a labile part E, with effective weight w = L + E. The optimiser updates w as usual, so standard training is unchanged; after each step the change is moved into E and the STC dynamics are applied:

BiologyNetwork
Early-phase LTP decaysE ← E · exp(−1/τE)
Synaptic tagh ← h · exp(−1/τh) + |Δw|
Plasticity-related products, shared per neuronp ← p · exp(−1/τp) + α·s(t)
Capturec = clamp(κ · p · [h > θ], 0, 1), then L += c·E, E −= c·E
Salient events(t): a pulse, or a sudden jump in training loss

Capture requires a tag and products at the same step, which creates a time window around the event; without an event every change decays. Ablations restrict products to tagged synapses and give each learning episode its own tag. Labile change is tracked per episode, so every consolidated change can be traced to its task and capturing event. The main measure is the consolidated share of a task's learning, Σ(consolidated · change) / Σ(change²), since accuracy saturates quickly.

Results

Setup: an MLP with two hidden layers of 256 units trained with SGD on two binary MNIST tasks (A: 3 vs 5, B: 4 vs 9), five seeds. Each run ends with 4000 steps without learning, so only consolidated memory remains. Multi-task experiments use a separate output head per task.

Event timing determines what is kept

Without an event, accuracy on A falls from 0.95 to 0.61; near the event it stays at about 0.95. As STC predicts, tag lifetime mainly shifts the after-side of the window and product lifetime the before-side. Since the shape follows from the time constants, this confirms the mechanism works inside gradient training. Without tags, an event at +600 steps still keeps 51%, and size-triggered capture keeps 62% with no event, leaving later events no influence.

Cross-task rescue

A salient onset of task B rescues task A retroactively; without salience neither is kept. With per-weight tags, B re-tags shared weights and still captures 24% of A at a 900-step gap; per-change tags give a clean window.

The consolidation log as an explanation

Faithfulness is evaluated with a deletion test: parameters are ranked by the log's attribution, Fisher importance, change size or at random, and the top 5% are reset to pre-learning values. Removing the log's task A parameters drops A from 0.87 to 0.80 while sparing B; removing B's drops B from 0.97 to 0.69 while sparing A. Fisher importance separates the memories far less clearly. Change size matches the log for A (0.796 against 0.797); the log does better for B (0.691 against 0.764).

Forgetting on Split-MNIST

Five binary tasks are learned in sequence. Surprise-gated variants fire an event when training loss exceeds its running mean by three standard deviations, without task labels.

STC alone does not measurably reduce forgetting, since consolidated weights are not protected from later learning. Using the consolidation log as importance in an EWC-style penalty reaches EWC level, with importance selected by surprising events after learning rather than fixed during it. It does not outperform Synaptic Intelligence.

Limitations

  • Small MLPs on MNIST only; the results demonstrate the mechanism, not performance at scale.
  • Time is measured in training steps, and time constants were set by hand.
  • The deletion test resets weights without retraining.
  • The surprise detector also reacts to loss noise, catching a task change within 20 steps in at most 3 of 4 cases.
  • Settings with delayed labels are not tested.
  • The biological mapping is simplified.
