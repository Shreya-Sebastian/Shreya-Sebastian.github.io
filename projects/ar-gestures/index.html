Personal project

AR gestures

Real-time hand tracking in the browser that maps five gestures to elemental effects anchored to the palm, with no build step and nothing to install.

Try it live. The demo uses your webcam; the video is processed in your browser and never leaves your device.

Open full screen ↗

Overview

A browser-based augmented reality demo driven by the webcam. MediaPipe Hands tracks 21 hand landmarks in real time, a rule-based recogniser classifies the gesture from their geometry, and a particle effect is rendered on a canvas overlay that follows the hand. It is written in plain JavaScript with Canvas 2D rendering and no build step.

GestureEffect
Open palmWind: particles orbiting the palm on three elliptical rings
FistEarth: rotating rocks and falling dust

Peace signFire: rising flame particles

Pipeline

camera -> MediaPipe landmarks -> gesture rules -> smoothing
       -> active effect -> palm anchor and scale -> particles

Tracking and rendering are decoupled: MediaPipe delivers landmarks asynchronously as camera frames are processed, while a separate animation loop redraws the canvas on every display frame from the latest landmarks. The camera image is mirrored and the landmarks flipped to match.

Gesture recognition

A finger counts as raised when its tip lies above its middle joint, and a pinch is detected from the distance between thumb and index tips. The remaining gestures follow from which fingers are raised. For temporal stability, the label is a majority vote over the last five frames, and a gesture must hold for 300 ms before its effect starts; an effect then persists until a different gesture is held.

Rendering

Effects are anchored to the palm centre, the mean of the wrist and the four finger bases, and scaled by the wrist to middle-finger-base distance, so sizes, speeds and orbit radii stay consistent as the hand moves towards or away from the camera. Each element is its own particle system with update and draw steps: fire uses short-lived particles with velocity damping, lightning regenerates recursive midpoint-displacement bolts drawn with a glow stroke and a thin core, earth combines orbiting fragments with gravity-driven dust, and ice draws procedural snowflakes. The frame time step is capped, dead particles are culled every frame and effect objects are reused, which keeps the animation smooth.
