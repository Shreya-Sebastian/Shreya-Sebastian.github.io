Personal project

Moneytrail

How far can you get from bank export files alone? A local tool that resolves merchants and detects recurring payments, without the data leaving your computer.

Overview

Moneytrail reads the CSV exports from four Dutch banks and reports who you pay and which payments repeat. It runs locally on DuckDB. The core problems are entity resolution (working out who a payment went to) and periodicity detection, both measured against labelled synthetic data.

Structure

bank export ─► importer ─► transactions ─► DuckDB ─► merchant resolution ─► recurring detection ─► report

Per-bank parsers map each format onto a common transaction schema with full provenance, so any result can be traced back to its line in the export. Each transaction gets a content-based id, which makes imports idempotent: re-imported files and overlapping exports do not create duplicates.

Merchant resolution

One shop shows up under many names: branch numbers, city suffixes, payment terminal prefixes, provider wrappers and names truncated at 22 characters. Each name is first normalised by stripping prefixes, legal forms, city names and tokens containing digits. The resulting keys are then clustered with union-find, merging first on a shared counterparty IBAN as the strongest signal and then on pairwise similarity: prefix and word containment, acronym matching and a RapidFuzz score of 90 or more. Each cluster is named after its longest readable variant used for a fair share of its payments.

Recurring payments

Payments are grouped by merchant, type and direction, split into amount bands and tested against weekly, four-weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly schedules. A band fits a schedule when at least three quarters of the gaps fall within its tolerance, and the best fit is the one with the smallest median error. Further rules recover a subscription hidden among one-off purchases at the same merchant by testing exact amounts separately, treat amounts within 3% as one price while detecting price changes on exact amounts, and handle stopped payments and two subscriptions under a single name.

Evaluation

A generator produces 18 months of synthetic transactions with ground-truth labels, in all four banks' formats. There are two sets: a development set written alongside the rules, and a held-out set written separately, without access to the matching code, with additional kinds of noise. The held-out set is used only for reporting and is replaced once the rules are changed in response to it.

Grouping is scored with B-cubed F1, which credits each transaction for how well its predicted cluster matches its true cluster. Results are over 6 random seeds and 4 bank formats, about 1,050 transactions per file.
